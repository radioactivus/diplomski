For your paper, I'd recommend organizing productivity evidence around AI by technique/application type, since that's where the research actually exists:

1. 🤖 Robotic Process Automation (RPA) → Task-Level Productivity

Bunduchi et al. (2025), "The Progressive Transformation of Work with RPA," European Journal of Information Systems (Taylor & Francis) https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0960085X.2025.2576231
Multi-case study of 3 organizations; RPA transforms work in 3 stages: convergent (efficiency), diffusing, divergent (new job designs)
Immediate gains: reduced errors, faster task execution; longer-term: role reconfiguration and workforce compositional shifts



2. 📊 Predictive Analytics / ML → Strategic Decision-Making

Adesina et al. (2024), "Leveraging Predictive Analytics for Strategic Decision-Making," World Journal of Advanced Research and Reviews (ResearchGate) https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381900026_Leveraging_predictive_analytics_for_strategic_decision-making_Enhancing_business_performance_through_data-driven_insights
Predictive AI improves profitability, efficiency, and market share through data mining, ML, and statistical models
Applies to finance (credit scoring, fraud), healthcare (patient outcomes), retail (demand forecasting)



3. 🏭 Computer Vision & Industrial AI → Manufacturing Productivity

Ettalibi et al. (2024), "AI and Computer Vision-based Real-time Quality Control," Procedia Computer Science (ScienceDirect)https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S187705092302207X
Barua et al. (2025), "Leveraging AI for Smart Production Management in Industry 4.0," Scientific Reports (Nature)https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-25413-6
Predictive maintenance, AI scheduling, CV quality control → measured reductions in downtime, improved throughput and first-pass yield



4. 💬 Generative AI (LLMs) → Knowledge Worker Productivity

Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2025), "Generative AI at Work" — Stanford. Customer service agents: +14% resolution rate, –25% handling time. Biggest gains for less-experienced workers. (Stanford)https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/augmenting-intelligence-the-effects-of-ai-on-productivity-and-work-practices/
Dell'Acqua, Mollick, Kellogg et al. (2023), "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" — Harvard/Wharton/MIT. 700+ BCG consultants: +40% performance inside AI's capability frontier; –19% outside it. (MIT Sloan)https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/how-generative-ai-can-boost-highly-skilled-workers-productivity

5. 📉 The Macro Paradox: Spending vs. Measured Output (Across All AI Types)

Baslandze et al. (2026), Atlanta Fed Working Paper — ~750 executives; perceived productivity gains > measured gains; a "productivity paradox." (Atlanta Fed)https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives
Opagio (2026) — $2.6T spent globally in 2025, 90% of firms report zero measurable impact; echoes Solow's 1987 computer paradox. Only 29% of execs can measure ROI. (Opagio)https://opag.io/ai/ai-productivity-paradox
Goldman Sachs (2024) — ~25% avg productivity increase in early adopters, but only ~5% of firms using AI in production. Expects GDP impact from 2027. (Goldman Sachs)https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/AI-is-showing-very-positive-signs-of-boosting-gdp
McKinsey — $2.6T–$4.4T potential annual economic impact, but only 1% of leaders call their rollouts "mature." (Summary)https://www.makebot.ai/blog-en/mckinsey-report-how-generative-ai-is-reshaping-global-productivity-and-the-future-of-work
